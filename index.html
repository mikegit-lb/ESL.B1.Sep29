CEFR B1 Speaking Success Guide

Master essential transition words, discourse markers, and understand what examiners look for at the B1 intermediate level.

🤔 Buying Time (Thinking Phrases)

Avoid awkward silences with natural opening fillers:

"Well, to be honest..." "That's an interesting question..." "Let me think for a moment..." "As far as I'm concerned..."

📌 Giving Reasons & Details

Never give a 1-word answer. Extend with examples:

"For instance / For example..." "The main reason why I say this is..." "In my personal experience..." "Another factor is that..."

⚖️ Showing Contrast & Nuance

Impress examiners by presenting both sides:

"On the one hand... but on the other hand..." "Although I usually enjoy..., sometimes I..." "However, compared to last year..." "Even though it's not ideal, I..."

🏁 Smooth Conclusions

Finish your response with confidence:

"All in all, I'd say that..." "To sum it up..." "So basically, that's my main routine." "Overall, it makes a huge difference."

📊 Cambridge B1 / PET Speaking Assessment Checklist

Criteria What B1 Level Expects How to Score High
Grammar & Vocabulary Accurate use of simple and compound tenses (Present Simple, Past Simple, Present Perfect, 'going to'). Use topic-specific words (e.g. *commute, procrastinate, extracurricular*) instead of basic words like *good/bad*.
Discourse Management Speaks in coherent sentences without excessive pausing or one-word answers. Connect sentences with linkers like *because, although, therefore, as well as*.
Pronunciation Intelligible and clear; individual sounds and sentence stress are mostly natural. Speak clearly at a moderate pace. Record yourself with the built-in recorder to check your clarity.
Interactive Communication Responds directly to the prompt and expands with relevant personal details. Answer the 'Why' and give a 2-3 sentence story or daily example for every prompt.
